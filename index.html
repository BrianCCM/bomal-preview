Landing page options
Culture Conference, November 2026

Three landing pages, three different conversion arguments.

Each one takes the same event, the same brand and the same facts, and builds a different case for registering. They differ in structure and in the objection they are designed to beat, not in decoration. Open them side by side and pick the argument that fits the traffic you are buying.

A

Direct response

The shortest path from ad click to registration. One screen, one decision, no detours. Everything a visitor needs to say yes is above the fold.

Best for

Warm traffic. People who already know the speaker and mostly need the date, the price and a button.

The objection it beats

Friction. Nobody abandons because they could not find the information.

Structure
  • 1Hero with speaker portrait and the single CTA
  • 2Four-fact strip: when, where, price, format
  • 3Speaker lineup
  • 4Three objection answers
  • 5Closing CTA band
Open option A With Wilcox ad

B

Authority wall

Leads with the people. Six portraits at full size before any sales copy, because for this event the lineup is the product.

Best for

Cold traffic that knows the speaker but has never heard of the foundation or the conference.

The objection it beats

Legitimacy. "Who is running this, and is it worth my Saturday?"

Structure
  • 1Short centered hero, one CTA
  • 2Six-portrait wall with roles and context
  • 3The institutions behind them
  • 4Walter Rane honoree spotlight
  • 5Live podcast taping, then CTA band
Open option B With Welch ad

C

Value stack

Itemizes everything the registration includes, line by line, then shows the price at the bottom so thirty dollars lands as a bargain rather than a cost.

Best for

Price-sensitive or comparison-shopping traffic, and anyone who has to justify the trip to a spouse.

The objection it beats

Cost and uncertainty. "What do I actually get for this?"

Structure
  • 1Hero with a sticky price panel
  • 2Itemized stack, including the paid add-ons
  • 3Two-day agenda
  • 4Refund policy as risk reversal
  • 5CTA band
Open option C With Pelo ad

What all three share

The conversion mechanics are identical across every option, so a test between them is a test of the argument and nothing else.

Speaker message-match

Add ?s=givens to any page and the hero portrait, name and role swap to the speaker whose ad was clicked, and that speaker drops out of the lineup grid below. The page the visitor lands on looks like the ad they just saw.

Self-managing urgency

One date in assets/lp.js drives the top strip and every price on the page. Before October 10 it counts down the early-bird discount. After it passes, the page flips itself to the event date and the add-on tickets. No edit needed.

Clean handoff and tracking

Every CTA goes to register.bomal.org carrying its UTM parameters, plus the speaker key as utm_content, so you can see which speaker's ad produced which registration. No navigation links leak traffic off the page.

Before these go live

Speaker quotes are empty on purpose. Nothing on these pages puts words in a speaker's mouth. If BOMAL supplies a line from each speaker, the quote slot is already wired into assets/lp.js.

Only six speakers have portraits. Those are the six published on bomal.org. Any other speaker running ads needs a headshot before their swap will work.

The event photos are real and now carry the social proof. Five shots from a previous conference, resized from roughly forty megabytes down to under a megabyte total so the pages still load fast on a phone over cell data. What is still missing is written proof: attendee quotes, last year's attendance, and how many years this has run.

Every fact here came from bomal.org and register.bomal.org on October 5, 2026: the dates, the venue, the thirty dollar price, the five dollar early-bird, the paid banquet and lunch add-ons, and the refund terms. Confirm them before spend goes live.
